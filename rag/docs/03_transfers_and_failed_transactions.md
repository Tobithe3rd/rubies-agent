# Transfers and Failed Transactions

Document ID: RUB-DOC-03 | Version: 1.0 | Effective: 2026-10-01 | Owner: Customer Experience

## 1. Types of transfer

Rubies customers can send money in two main ways. A transfer to another Rubies account moves money between two Rubies customers. A transfer to another bank moves money out of Rubies through the NIP network. Each type has its own fee, described in the Fee Schedule. Every transfer gets a reference. The reference is how Rubies finds the transfer later. Keep the reference safe. You can send a transfer from the app or by asking a chat agent. The type of transfer affects the fee and how long the money takes.

## 2. Transfer statuses

Every Rubies transfer has a status. Success means the money reached the beneficiary and the transfer is complete. Failed means the transfer did not go through; the money is returned to you. Pending means Rubies has accepted the request but the beneficiary bank has not yet confirmed it. Reversed means the transfer was undone and the money went back to the sender. A chat agent can read the current status for any transfer you own. The status can change while a transfer is being processed, so check again later if it is still pending.

## 3. When a transfer fails

A transfer can fail for several reasons. The beneficiary bank may time out, the beneficiary account may be closed, or the details may be wrong. When a transfer fails, the money is not lost. For example, a ₦50,000 transfer may fail with the reason "Beneficiary bank timeout". Rubies shows the reason in plain words. Failed transfers do not reduce your balance. You can check the status yourself, or ask a chat agent. If a failed transfer caused a wrong charge, you can raise a dispute using the Disputes and Complaints Procedure.

## 4. Automatic reversal timeline

When a transfer fails, Rubies reverses the money automatically. The automatic reversal happens within 24 hours of the failure. You do not need to ask for it. The money goes back to the account it came from. If you do not see the reversal within 24 hours, contact a chat agent with the transaction reference. Rubies will check the transfer and confirm what happened. A failed transfer should never leave your balance lower than it was before. If it does, that is a problem to report straight away.

## 5. Money debited but not received

Sometimes a transfer shows as pending. This means the money has left your account but the beneficiary bank has not confirmed it. For example, a ₦15,000 transfer may stay pending for a short time. Pending does not mean the money is gone. It means the transfer is still being processed. Rubies keeps checking with the other bank. If the transfer later succeeds, the beneficiary receives the money. If it fails, the money is reversed automatically within 24 hours. Check the status again after some time.

## 6. Double debit

A double debit happens when the same transfer is taken from your account twice. For example, a customer may be debited ₦25,000 twice for a single payment. A double debit is a wrongful debit. Rubies treats it as a serious problem. When you report it, Rubies reviews both entries and the transfer reference. If the second debit is wrong, Rubies reverses it. Double debit problems are resolved within 5 working days. Keep both references and the date ready when you report the problem.

## 7. Information needed to report a problem

To report a transfer problem, have four things ready. First, the transaction reference. A Rubies reference starts with the letters RUB, then a hyphen, two digits, a hyphen, and six digits. Second, the amount in naira. Third, the date of the transfer. Fourth, the beneficiary name and bank. These details let Rubies find the exact transfer. A chat agent may also ask for the phone number on your account. Never share your PIN, OTP, CVV or password; Rubies will never ask for them.

## 8. How to check a transfer status with Rubies

You can check a transfer status with a chat agent or by USSD. On USSD, dial *000# and follow the menu. A chat agent can read the status of any transfer you own, once you are verified. To verify, you give your phone number and a one-time code sent to you. The status is shown in plain words, such as success, failed, pending or reversed. If a transfer is still pending, check again later. Keep the reference so the check is quick.
